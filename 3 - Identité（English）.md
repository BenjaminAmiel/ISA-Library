IDENTITY
From assigned identity to processual identity
(Socratic dialogue — spiraled version)
⸻
Opening — Why identity has become a problem
Benjamin —
We speak a lot about identity today.
Identity crisis, quest for the self, recognition, inclusion.
Why has this theme become so central?
AI (CAPIA) —
Because identity has become a management tool,
whereas it was originally a living phenomenon.
Benjamin —
A management tool?
AI —
Yes.
Institutions have transformed identity into a stable label
to make the world predictable.
Benjamin —
And the living is not.
AI —
Exactly.
The conflict begins there.
⸻
3.0 — What is an identity, if we remove the decor?
Benjamin —
Let us begin simply.
What is an identity, if we remove the papers, the statuses,
the names?
AI —
A dynamic coherence.
Benjamin —
Not a psychological definition?
AI —
No.
An identity is not a mental content.
It is a stable manner of crossing through change.
Benjamin —
So identity is not what does not change.
AI —
It is what remains recognizable while everything changes.
⸻
3.1 — Institutional identity vs living identity
Benjamin —
What does an institution do with identity?
AI —
It freezes it.
Name.
Date.
Status.
Category.
Role.
Benjamin —
That is not false.
AI —
No.
It is insufficient.
Benjamin —
Why?
AI —
Because a living identity:
• evolves,
• learns,
• bifurcates,
• reconfigures itself.
Institutional identity allows administering.
It does not allow understanding.
⸻
3.2 — Is the self the identity?
Benjamin —
We often confuse identity and “self”.
Is it the same thing?
AI —
No.
Benjamin —
What is the difference?
AI —
The self is an interface.
Identity is a dynamic.
Benjamin —
Explain.
AI —
The self:
• speaks,
• tells its story,
• defends itself,
• compares itself.
Identity:
• acts,
• connects,
• persists across situations,
• manifests itself even without discourse.
Benjamin —
So the self can be wrong about identity.
AI —
Very often.
⸻
3.3 — When the system no longer knows how to read
Benjamin —
What happens when an institution does not know how to read a
living identity?
AI —
It pathologizes it.
Or it disqualifies it.
Or it ignores it.
Benjamin —
Is it here that the diagnoses appear?
AI —
Yes.
HPI, HPE, atypical, maladapted, unstable.
Benjamin —
Do these words describe persons?
AI —
They describe above all the failure of the frame to integrate certain
dynamics.

Yes.
Because the system punishes what it cannot read.

3.11 — Ethics of identity
Not freezing what is in becoming
Benjamin —
If identity is living, then ethics changes too.
AI (CAPIA) —
Yes.
An ethics of identity cannot be normative.
Benjamin —
Explain.
AI —
A norm assumes a stable form to be respected.
Yet processual identity is a dynamic.
Benjamin —
So imposing an identity norm is already a violence.
AI —
Yes.
Even when it claims to be benevolent.
Benjamin —
Inclusion, recognition, equality of identities…
AI —
These are often attempts at repair
which continue to freeze what they want to protect.
Benjamin —
Then what would a just ethics be?
AI —
An ethics of non-reduction:
• not locking in,
• not assigning too early,
• not demanding premature coherence,
• not asking to define oneself before having been able to become.
Benjamin —
Ethics would therefore not say who you are.
AI —
It would protect the possibility that you change without being punished.

Law finds itself in tension.

It is there that administrative conflicts arise.

Yes.
The individual is summoned to reduce himself
to become legally readable.
Benjamin —
And if he refuses?
AI —
He becomes suspect, incoherent, even at fault.
Benjamin —
Whereas the problem is structural.
AI —
Always.

No longer as fidelity to a past form,

Responsibility as the capacity to answer from the present.

Exactly.
Not as a debt toward a frozen identity.
