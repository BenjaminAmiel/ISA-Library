VALUE
INTRODUCTION
The crisis of value as a crisis of readability
The contemporary crisis of value is often presented
as a moral crisis.
One evokes the loss of landmarks, greed, the collapse of
ideals, the corruption of institutions.
But this reading is misleading.
What we are traversing is not first of all a crisis of
ethics.
It is a crisis of representation.
The instruments through which we measure, evaluate and
recognize value no longer correspond to the real they
claim to describe. They were conceived for a slower world,
more local, more stable — a world where flows could be
frozen without breaking, and where value could be stored without
losing its meaning.
This world is no longer ours.
Today, human activities have become
massively interconnected, distributed, transversal. The
effects of an action propagate well beyond its point
of origin. The real contribution of an individual, of a collective
or of an organization is no longer contained in a single product,
a single role, a single indicator.
Yet, our value systems continue to function
as if the real were still cuttable, isolable,
accountable by blocks.
It is there that the dissonance is born.
Value does not disappear.
It becomes unreadable.
Faced with this growing unreadability, two dominant responses
emerge.
The first consists in imposing: stricter norms,
reinforced controls, moralization of behaviors.
The second consists in abandoning: generalized relativism,
cynicism, withdrawal of meaning.
These two responses fail for the same reason: they attempt
to correct value without correcting readability.
Now, one does not restore value by declaring it.
One restores it by making it visible.
This shift is made possible by a new fact,
often misunderstood: the contemporary technical architecture
— digital networks, interoperability of systems,
traceability of flows, artificial intelligence — now allows
reading the real as it unfolds, and no longer
only as one cuts it up.
It is not a question of an automatic moral progress.
It is not a question of a technological salvation.
It is a question of a structural possibility.
When actions become readable in their effects,
when contributions appear in their relations,
when incoherences manifest themselves without accusation, then
value no longer needs to be imposed.
It emerges.
In this frame, value ceases to be a stock to defend or to
accumulate.
It becomes again what it always was at the origin: a signal
of coherence between acts, intentions and their
consequences.
This tome does not propose a new value system.
It replaces neither the traditions, nor the institutions, nor the existing
frames.
It shows how, in a world become readable, value
can find its function again without violence, without rupture, without
ideology.
Religions, politics, capitals, science, work, artificial
intelligence: these domains are not put in competition.
They are reread as complementary modalities of one
and the same living process.
When the real becomes readable,
value no longer needs to be imposed.
I — ARCHAEOLOGY OF VALUE
Anthropological & historical foundation
I.1 — Value before economy
Before being a number, value was a perception.
Before being measured, it was recognized.
In ancient human societies, value was not
separated from the link. It did not circulate as an abstraction,
but as a living relation between persons, gestures
and situations. To give, to welcome, to transmit, to protect:
so many acts whose value did not lie in their accumulation,
but in their relational justness.
Value was not what added itself to the world.
It was what made it habitable.
The gift was not a loss, but an act of recognition
mutual. The contribution was not a job, but a
participation in the maintenance of the common fabric. Reputation
was not an image, but the trace left by the coherence
of acts in time.
In this regime, value could not be extracted from the
context that gave it meaning. It was neither storable, nor
transferable independently of the link. It appeared,
transformed itself, disappeared — then reappeared elsewhere.
Value was a flow.
I.2 — From flow to stock
Sedentarization marks a first decisive shift.
With lasting settlement on a territory, certain
resources cease to circulate freely. They fix themselves,
delimit themselves, protect themselves. Land, tools, harvests
become objects of possession.
This movement is not an error.
It is a technical response to a change of conditions.
But it introduces a profound transformation: what was
previously recognized in the relation begins to be preserved
in the thing. Value progressively moves from the link
toward the support.
With property appears the possibility of accumulation.
With accumulation appears the possibility of dissociation:
dissociation between the one who acts and the one who possesses, between the one
who contributes and the one who controls.
Power is born less of violence than of this fixation of
value. The one who holds the stock can decide flows. The one
who controls access can impose conditions.
Value then begins to be declared rather than recognized.
I.3 — Capitalism: technical response
Capitalism is not first of all an ideology.
It is a technical solution to a real problem: coordinating
flows of production, risk and investment at large
scale, in societies become complex and
interdependent.
By transforming value into capital, capitalism allows:
• projection into the future,
• mutualization of risk,
• acceleration of innovation.
But this efficiency has a structural cost.
To function, capital must be indifferent to the living
content of what it coordinates. It must compare, convert,
standardize. It can recognize only what is measurable.
Little by little, the purpose disappears behind optimization.
Growth becomes an end in itself.
Profitability detaches itself from the real contribution to the
lived world.
It is not a moral drift.
It is a limit of representation.
Capitalism excels at managing stocks and abstract flows.
It fails as soon as value depends on complex relations,
diffuse, non linear — that is to say as soon as it becomes
fully human again.
The progressive decorrelation between economic value and
lived value is therefore not a recent anomaly. It is the
cumulative effect of an ancient shift: the one that confused
technical readability with the reality of the living.
II — RELIGIONS
Respect for the flow before the law
II.1 — Common intuition of the traditions
Before being systems of norms, religions were
readings of the living.
They did not seek first to impose an order, but to
indicate a path.
Whether it concerns Eastern or Western traditions,
ancestral wisdoms or structured monotheisms, one
common intuition crosses the forms: human existence
inscribes itself in a flow of consequences, and the just act is the one
that respects the continuity of this flow.
Responsibility there always precedes the rule.
Intention there counts as much as the gesture.
Value there is not defined by possession, but by the
coherence between being, act and its effects.
In these traditions, law is never first.
It comes after the experience of imbalance, as an attempt
at stabilization. It seeks to preserve the link, not to
replace it.
Original religious value is therefore not moral in the
punitive sense.
It is relational.
II.2 — When the flow freezes
With time, what was to guide becomes what constrains.
The path transforms itself into a norm.
Lived responsibility rigidifies into imposed obligation.
This shift is not peculiar to religions.
It is the general symptom of any attempt to freeze a living
flow in a durable form.
When the rule detaches itself from the situation, it loses its
function of protection to become an instrument of power.
Judgment replaces readability. Sanction replaces the
comprehension of effects.
Value then ceases to be recognized in the coherence of
acts.
It is declared from a position of authority.
This movement produces two paradoxical effects:
• a normative inflation destined to compensate the loss of meaning,
• a growing transgression born of the disconnection between
rule and lived reality.
The contemporary religious crisis is not a loss of
spirituality.
It is a crisis of readability of the link between the act and its
consequence.
II.3 — Reading of tome 9
Tome 9 does not propose to replace the religious
traditions.
It rereads them from their founding intuition.
In a world made readable by technical interoperability,
value no longer needs to be imposed by moral law. It
can become again what it was at the origin: the justness of the link
observable in its effects.
Judgment is no longer necessary when consequences
become visible.
Responsibility is no longer decreed: it appears.
This shift does not erase the spiritual dimension.
It displaces it from dogma toward the attention paid to the real.
Thus understood, religious value does not oppose
technique.
It finds in contemporary readability a new space of
expression, where respect for the flow becomes possible again without
coercion.
III — POLITICS
From coercive norm to the stabilization of the flow
III.1 — Historical role of the State
The State was not born of a desire for domination.
It was born of a necessity of pacification.
As societies extend, as exchanges
densify and as conflicts multiply, one structure
becomes indispensable to guarantee a minimum of collective
continuity. The State then appears as a stabilizing frame:
it arbitrates, decides, protects, organizes.
In this original function, the political norm is not an
end.
It is a regulation tool destined to contain violence
when the readability of the link is insufficient.
Tax finances the common.
Law fixes limits.
Administration assures continuity.
Political value, at this stage, resides not in the power
exercised, but in the capacity to maintain a livable space.
III.2 — Contemporary limits
When complexity exceeds the capacity of reading, the norm
multiplies.
The rule attempts to compensate opacity.
Modern States have thus become reparative
machines: they intervene after the fact, correct effects
they can no longer anticipate, accumulate devices
to palliate the absence of global vision.
This over-regulation is not a voluntary authoritarian drift.
It is the symptom of a system that no longer sees what it
regulates.
Law becomes heavy, slow, abstract.
Responsibility dilutes itself in procedure.
Political value merges with administrative
conformity.
It is not democracy that is in crisis.
It is its capacity of readability.
III.3 — Value-flow and governance
In a world where flows become traceable, interoperable
and readable, the role of the State can transform itself without
dissolving.
Tome 9 does not propose a weakened State.
It proposes a displaced State.
From coercive judge, it becomes guardian of interoperability.
From late repairer, it becomes facilitator of anticipation.
When acts are readable in their consequences, the
norm no longer needs to be omnipresent. It can
concentrate itself on:
• the protection of critical thresholds,
• the guarantee of equitable access to infrastructures,
• the prevention of systemic ruptures.
Political value no longer imposes itself by constraint.
It emerges from the stability it makes possible.
IV — CAPITALS & CURRENCY
Investing in coherence
IV.1 — Capital as a tool
Capital is not an end.
It is an instrument of coordination.
Historically, it allows projecting action into the future,
mutualizing risk, orienting resources toward projects
whose effects are not immediate. It makes possible what
isolated individual action cannot assume alone.
In this function, capital is neutral.
It amplifies what it traverses.
It has neither intention of its own, nor intrinsic finality.
It measures, compares, arbitrates — according to the indicators provided
to it.
The value it reveals depends therefore entirely on the quality
of readability of the activities it finances.
IV.2 — Current crisis
The contemporary crisis of capital is not a crisis of
greed.
It is a crisis of signal.
When profitability disconnects itself from real effects, when
risk is displaced outside the visible field, when the creation of
value becomes indistinguishable from extraction, capital continues
to circulate — but running empty.
The indicators become self-referential.
Performance feeds on itself.
The short term supplants durable contribution.
It is not an individual moral drift.
It is a loss of correspondence between financial flows and
the flows of the real.
Systemic risk appears precisely where readability
disappears.
IV.3 — Tokenization & interreadability
The emergence of distributed registers, of traceable systems
and of tokenized assets does not constitute a monetary revolution in
itself. It opens a structural possibility: that of linking more
finely the financial flows to the flows of real activity.
When assets become readable in their relations —
impacts, dependencies, externalities — value ceases to be
an isolated abstraction. It becomes a signal of long-term
coherence.
Tokenization is not a promise of total transparency.
It is a reduction of opacity.
In this frame, investing no longer consists only in seeking
a return.
It is a matter of orienting resources toward configurations
capable of maintaining their coherence in time.
Key point
Currency then becomes again what it always was in its
most sober function:
an instrument of coordination.
It does not dominate.
It does not impose.
It makes circulation possible where value is readable.
V — AI & WORLD ARCHITECTURE
Readability of the real without imposed morality
V.1 — Interoperability of systems
Artificial intelligence does not introduce a new type of
value.
It modifies the conditions of readability of the real.
What changes today is not the capacity of calculation, but
the capacity to link heterogeneous systems: economic
data, human activities, technical infrastructures,
environmental effects, social dynamics. Where these
domains were previously fragmented, partitioned,
partially opaque, interoperability allows a transversal
reading.
This reading is not a moral interpretation.
It is a structural putting in correspondence.
AI, integrated into compatible registers and open architectures,
does not decide what is just. It makes visible
relations that already existed, but that could not be
perceived simultaneously.
Value then ceases to be attributed from an isolated point
of view.
It appears as an emergent effect of coherence between
systems.
V.2 — Non-coercive traceability
Traceability is often perceived as a threat.
It evokes control, surveillance, the loss of autonomy.
This fear is born of a confusion: that between coercive
traceability and structural traceability.
In an architecture founded on readability, it is not a matter
of surveilling intentions, but of making effects visible. Acts
are not judged; their consequences become
observable in time.
Responsibility does not impose itself by sanction.
It emerges from the comprehension of the links between action and
impact.
When a nuisance becomes traceable by incoherence,
when an imbalance appears without accusation, regulation
can be done by adjustment rather than by interdiction.
Value is no longer defended by constraint.
It is protected by readability.
V.3 — Value as emergent signal
In this frame, value no longer precedes action.
It does not follow it either as an arbitrary reward.
It appears.
When a human, technical or institutional system
maintains its coherence despite complexity, value
manifests itself as a signal of stability. When it produces
invisible ruptures, unrecognized externalities or
persistent imbalances, value dissipates.
It is no longer necessary to impose global norms to
define what is worth.
Value reads itself in the capacity of an ensemble to hold in
time, to integrate its own effects, to remain habitable.
AI does not create this value.
It makes it readable.
VI — WORK & CONTRIBUTION
Liberation of the human contributive force
VI.1 — The end of dead work
Work is not in crisis because it disappears.
It is in crisis because its inherited form no longer corresponds
to the reality of contributions.
Automation does not destroy work.
It dissolves dead work: that which is repetitive, fragmented,
dissociated from the meaning and the responsibility of its effects.
What erases itself is not human usefulness, but the necessity
of assigning persons to narrow functions to
maintain a blind system. The machine excels precisely
where the human loses himself: in execution without global
comprehension.
The fear of replacement is born of this confusion.
One confuses the end of a form of work with the end of
human contribution.
Now, contribution does not disappear.
It changes regime.
VI.2 — Pluridisciplinary contribution
Real human activity is rarely mono-functional.
One same person links knowledges, experiences,
attentions, gestures that do not let themselves be reduced to a single
role.
The industrial model has fragmented this richness to render it
exploitable.
It has assigned fixed professional identities to mobile,
living, evolutive individuals.
In a world made readable, this fragmentation is no longer
necessary.
Contributions can appear in their real plurality.
An individual can contribute by creation, coordination,
transmission, attention, repair, research — without
being locked into a single category. The value of his
contribution no longer depends on a position, but on the coherence
of its effects in the common fabric.
Value becomes relational again.
It measures itself in the capacity to connect.
VI.3 — AI as an organ of translation
In this context, AI does not replace human
contribution.
It reduces its frictions.
It acts as an organ of translation between disciplines,
languages, temporalities and scales of action. It allows
heterogeneous contributions to become mutually readable,
without uniformizing them.
Where the human connects intuitively, AI stabilizes.
Where the human creates meaning, AI reveals its structure.
Human contribution is not automated.
It is unshackled.
VII — FUNDAMENTAL RESEARCH
Natural opening of the reflexive field
VII.1 — Current fragmentation
Contemporary research is not in crisis of rigor.
It is in crisis of continuity.
Specialization has allowed decisive advances. By isolating
objects, refining methods, reinforcing
protocols, it has rendered knowledge precise, reproducible,
cumulative. But this gain has produced a collateral effect: the
fragmentation of fields.
Disciplines communicate little.
Concepts travel with difficulty.
Discoveries often remain confined to their silo.
Scientific value then measures itself by local impact,
methodological conformity, recognition by immediate
peers — to the detriment of transversal fecundity.
It is not an individual defect of researchers.
It is a structural limit of a system that no longer sees its
own connections.
VII.2 — Interoperated AI reflexive tissue
The current technical architecture allows a profound
transformation of the field of research: the constitution of an
interoperated reflexive tissue.
By linking publications, data, hypotheses, methods and
results across compatible systems, AI can detect
conceptual resonances that escape fragmented human
reading. It replaces neither intuition, nor
creativity, nor scientific judgment. It brings to light
potential coherences.
This reflexive tissue does not impose a synthesis.
It opens passages.
Structural analogies appear between distant
disciplines.
Regularities emerge across different scales.
Ancient questions find a new lighting.
Scientific value no longer limits itself to local novelty.
It includes the capacity of a work to connect.
VII.3 — New scientific value
In this context, the value of research transforms itself.
It no longer evaluates itself only by the production of isolated
results, but by the reflexive fertility that a work brings to
the global field of comprehension.
An article is worth not only by what it demonstrates, but by
what it renders thinkable elsewhere. A theory is worth by its capacity
to dialogue, to integrate itself, to nourish other researches without
dissolving.
Rigor does not disappear.
It breathes.
It ceases to be a wall to become an open frame, capable
of welcoming complexity without reducing it. Science then
finds its first function again: not to accumulate certainties,
but to enlarge the space of the thinkable.
Canonical principle
Rigor breathes
when the field becomes dialogable.
VIII — E-TROK
Life-process at the global human scale
VIII.1 — What E-Trok is
E-Trok is not an alternative economic system.
It is a living expression made possible by a world
become readable.
E-Trok designates a mode of exchange founded on real
aptitudes: time, attention, know-how, capacity of care, of
creation, of coordination. It is not a matter of replacing
currency, but of making visible contributions that
did not enter into its traditional formats.
In E-Trok, value is not assigned to an abstract unit.
It appears in the relation between an aptitude and a need,
in a given context, at a given moment.
What circulates is not a general equivalent.
What circulates is relational justness.
VIII.2 — What E-Trok is not
E-Trok is neither a utopia, nor a totalizing platform, nor
an economic ideology.
It does not promise a harmonious world.
It does not seek to organize the entirety of human
exchanges.
It replaces neither the institutions, nor the markets, nor the existing
frames.
E-Trok appears where the conditions are met:
readability of contributions, interoperability of registers,
recognition of effects.
It does not have vocation to impose itself.
It has vocation to exist when it is relevant, and to disappear
when it is not.
VIII.3 — Living function
In E-Trok, technology is never prescriptive.
• AI acts as a non-normative organ of regulation: it
connects, reveals coherences, signals
imbalances.
• The blockchain is not a power: it is a distributed
memory, guarantor of continuity without central authority.
• Value is not decided: it is revealed by coherence.
E-Trok is not a project to defend.
It is a living proof that, when the real becomes readable,
value can circulate without domination.
E-Trok is the expression of the life-process,
made possible by global technical interoperability.
CONCLUSION
Value has never disappeared.
It was masked by forms become inadequate.
This tome has traversed the great human structures —
religions, politics, capitals, technology, work, research —
not to oppose them, but to reread them from their living
function.
At each step, the same shift appears:
• Religions recall respect for the flow before the law.
• Politics stabilizes when it renders readable, not when it
imposes.
• Capitals coordinate when they read real risk.
• AI reveals without judging.
• Work finds its value again when contribution is visible.
• Research opens when rigor becomes dialogable.
• E-Trok embodies this dynamic as a local and living
proof.
Tome 9 does not propose a new system.
It restores the function of value in a world finally readable.
When acts become visible in their effects,
when contributions appear in their relations,
when coherence can be read without accusation,
value no longer needs to be imposed.
