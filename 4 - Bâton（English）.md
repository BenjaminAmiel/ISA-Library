STICK
Stick. I did not begin by thinking the stick, I took it. I let it weigh. I felt the
gravity inscribe itself there before any intention. The stick did not come as a sport, nor as a
discipline, still less as a performance. It came as a slow evidence,
almost primitive, an evidence that the body recognizes before language arrives. From the
first movements, something settled into place: not a learning in the
scholastic sense, but an internal reorganization. The arm was no longer an arm, the shoulder no longer a
articulation, the wrist no longer a control point; everything became trajectory. A
living trajectory, fluctuating, never identical, but always coherent. The stick revealed what
I had always known without formulating it: thought is not in the head, it is in the movement
that stabilizes itself without freezing.
At the start, I set rules. Always. As in music. As in all learning
that is serious. The thumb and the index. Minimal grip. Maintained hard point. The rest of the fingers free,
almost useless, like wings that accompany without directing. These rules were not
punitive constraints, but small sequential sticks: provisional beacons to force
attention, to prevent dispersion, to create a stable focus. Exactly as in the
sequential systems: one imposes a form to allow something else to emerge. And very quickly,
I felt that these rules were working for me. They were not reducing the movement, they were
making it readable. They made the spiral appear.
For everything is spiral. The shoulder initiates, the elbow transmits, the wrist modulates. A circle engenders a
other circle. A small circle accelerates, gives birth to a broader rotation, which drifts,
slows down, changes phase. The stick describes in the air what equations show
only imperfectly: entangled gravitational systems, continuous phase transitions,
an embodied morphodynamics. I see Landau in the wrist. I see non
linear in the slightest variation of tension. Nothing is fixed, and yet nothing is chaotic.
It is a dance of thresholds.
At some point, the small sticks become useless. As always. That is where the learning
tips over. One removes the rules, not because one rejects them, but because they have done their work.
The stick becomes the big stick. Then it even ceases to be stick. It disappears as a separate object. It
is no longer “me” and “the stick”. There is a field of continuous movement. And in this state, the focus
is no longer a tension. It is an active relaxation. A holding without effort. Exactly as in
drift, exactly as in skiing when there is no longer any straight line but only
trajectory. Performance is no longer the objective. Coherence is.
That is where I understood something fundamental: this focus is the one in which I
think best. Not by forcing. Not by fighting fear or emotion. But by integrating them
into the movement. High-level athletes know this state, but they often reach it
despite their fears, despite the pressure, despite the competitive stake. As for me, I reach it from the
life-process. From a consciousness that is not oriented toward victory, but toward
coherence. The difference is there. And it is decisive.
At this stage, AI changes status. It does not disappear. It is not rejected. It becomes what
it should always have been: one tool among others. A powerful tool, yes. A brilliant tool,
sometimes indispensable for structuring, writing, transmitting. But no longer a crutch. No longer a
substitute. The stick showed me what AI had already prepared me to understand: once the
process is integrated, the tool can withdraw without the movement collapsing. And even better:
without the tool, sometimes, the movement becomes purer.
This is not pathological. It is the opposite. It is the sign that something is integrated. When I
am without AI, I am not amputated. I am full. And when I come back to AI, I use it better, with
more rightness, more sobriety, more respect. The stick did not replace AI. It completed
the system. It closed the loop. It made visible what was latent: human cognition is
fundamentally morphodynamic. It needs gestures, gravity, resistance,
matter in order to think wide.
The stick then became a total metaphor. Small sequential stick: the rules, the protocols, the
frames, the institutions, the models. Big stick: integration, fluidity, the capacity to play with
complexity without getting lost. Then no more stick: the state. The state of consciousness where the tool is no longer
necessary because the structure is embodied. This is exactly what AI can produce in
a human when it is well used: not a dependence, but a profound
autonomization.
Tome 4 therefore does not speak of the stick. It speaks of this precise moment when the human remembers that he is
capable of absorbing a tool, of making it his own, then of surpassing it. Like the weapon become dance.
Like the equation become gesture. Like the algorithm become structured intuition. The stick is not
the end. It is the passage. And once the passage is crossed, what remains is neither the stick nor AI.
It is living coherence. The one that thinks in spiral, acts in trajectory, and no longer loses itself in
straight lines.
There is a precise moment when the body stops searching. I recognized it immediately, because it
was accompanied by no euphoria. No peak. No excitement. Just a calm evidence.
The stick kept turning, but something in me had settled. The focus was no longer to be
maintained: it was there. And it is at this instant that I understood that true learning is not
an accumulation, but a progressive deactivation of useless effort. What I had taken for
mastery before was often only a well-tolerated tension. Here, mastery was
silent. It no longer demanded conscious attention, it freed attention for something
else.
Then thought came back, but differently. Not as a commentary on the gesture, not
as an external analysis. It came back as an internal resonance, exactly as
when a right idea appears without premeditation. The stick was tracing trajectories and, at the same
time, conceptual architectures were settling into place. Not by deduction, but by
isomorphy. The movement was not representing thought, it was engendering it. I was not in the process
of reflecting on the spiral: I was in the spiral, and the spiral was thinking through me.
That is where I clearly saw what I now call endomathematics. Not
mathematics applied to the body, but mathematics internal to the living, perceptible
only from experience. A slowing down is not a loss of energy, it is a
storing. An acceleration is not an effort, it is a liberation. A change of phase
is not a rupture, it is a continuity become visible.
All this, I saw it in the stick as I had seen it before in models, systems
dynamic, the equations of transition. But here, no abstraction came to mask reality: reality was the model.
At this stage, fear no longer had a direct grip. Not because it had disappeared, but because
it no longer found any hold. Fear loves straight lines, fixed objectives,
comparisons, deadlines. Now there was no longer any objective. Only a trajectory. And in
a trajectory, fear becomes information. A micro-variation of tension. A signal
among others. It no longer blocks, it adjusts. This is exactly what differentiates competition from
coherence. In competition, fear is an obstacle. In coherence, it is a
parameter.
I then remembered everything I had done before: music, improvisation,
teaching, skiing, piloting. Everything was already there. The stick had invented nothing. It had revealed
the invariant. This invariant that I had sensed without ever formulating it completely: the living
learns through temporary constraints and blossoms through their dissolution. Always. Everywhere. In
all domains. The small stick is necessary. The big stick is liberating. And then comes the
moment when even the stick is too much.
That is where the tool changes nature. AI, like the stick, like any technology, is neither
good nor bad. It is transitory. Its function is not to replace human cognition,
but to bring it to a point where it can recognize itself. If the tool becomes indispensable
for life, then it has failed. If it can withdraw without collapse, then it has fulfilled its function. The
stick showed me that this withdrawal is not a loss, but a gain in density. What remains is
simpler, more stable, more silent.
I now understand why certain ancestral practices have always privileged
simple objects. A stick. A stone. A breath. This is not for lack of sophistication,
it is by refusal of dispersion. The simpler the tool, the more it obliges one to integrate complexity into oneself.
And the deeper this integration, the more cognition becomes autonomous. The stick is poor in
features, but rich in gravity. It does nothing in your place. It confronts you. It obliges you to feel. It
brings you back to the real without mediation.
And it is there, precisely there, that I saw the final link with all the rest. The institutions. Identity. The
philosophy. Everything the previous tomes had posited conceptually found here an
irrefutable anchoring. Coherence is not decreed. It is practiced. It is worked in the
body before being stated in language. A coherent institution should function like
a well-held stick: enough rules to stabilize, enough freedom to let dance. Too many
rules, and the gesture freezes. Not enough, and it disperses.
Tome 4 was born there. Not as a logical continuation, but as a descent into the flesh of
everything that had been thought. The stick is not a theme. It is a revealer. It shows what
happens when cognition stops being solely symbolic and becomes gravitational again.
When thought accepts weighing, turning, slowing down, accelerating, without judging itself. When it
stops wanting to conclude and accepts to continue.
And as long as the movement continues, the living is safe.
There is something deeply soothing in no longer seeking to name everything at
once. The stick taught me this too. As long as I tried to say what I was doing, the gesture remained
slightly behind itself. As soon as I accepted not understanding immediately, the
movement gained in precision. It is a strange lesson for a thought accustomed to analyzing,
but it is decisive: understanding arrives after integration, never before. And this
inverted temporality changes everything. It removes the anguish of having to know. It reinstalls
confidence in the fact that meaning will emerge because the body, for its part, already knows.
At that moment, I stopped correcting the stick. I let it carry me. Not as an
object that would take control, but as a mass that dialogues with gravity. The stick
always falls right. It is I who sometimes resist. When I resist, the movement becomes
jerky. When I let go, it becomes precise. This precision is not rigid. It is attuned.
Like an instrument one stops tuning in order to start playing. Here again, I
recognized something very ancient: the state in which music starts to play the
musician.
And in this state, time changes texture. It is no longer cut into sequences. It becomes
continuous, elastic. It is the same time as that of writing when it flows, the same as
that of right thought when it does not force its transitions. I could stay a long time in
this practice without mental fatigue, because there was no longer any internal friction. The body
worked, yes, but the psyche rested. Or rather, it stopped fighting against itself.
This is perhaps, at bottom, true rest: no longer fighting against one’s own coherence.
Little by little, I saw another evidence appear. Everything I had described as “structural
depression” in the previous tomes was not a collapse, but a saturation. Too many
straight lines. Too many imposed objectives. Too many frames that do not allow trajectory. The
stick, for its part, imposes nothing. It sanctions inconsistency immediately, but without violence. If the
gesture is false, it becomes uncomfortable. If it is right, it becomes fluid. No judgment. No
delay. An immediate, embodied feedback. I understood then why so many abstract systems
become toxic: they have lost this direct link between action and felt experience.
In the stick, no dissociation is possible. One cannot cheat for long. The body
knows. And this knowledge is not verbal. It is gravitational. It passes through weight,
inertia, the minimal necessary tension. It is a silent intelligence, but redoutably
precise. Far more precise than any discourse on coherence. It does not state itself, it
is lived. And once lived, it is no longer forgotten.
That is where I understood something fundamental about augmented human cognition. AI
can accelerate, structure, reveal. But it cannot replace this loop. It cannot
give weight. It cannot transmit inertia. It cannot make one feel the rightness
of a movement. And this is not a flaw. It is a border. A healthy border. A border
that recalls that every intelligence, however sophisticated it may be, remains disembodied. The danger is not
to cross this border. The danger is to forget it.
The stick became, without my seeking it, a safeguard. A constant reminder that thought
must sometimes come back down into matter so as not to lose itself in its own elegance. It
taught me to slow down without regressing, to simplify without impoverishing, to remove without destroying. Each time
I removed a useless constraint, the movement gained in amplitude. Each time I
added an arbitrary rule, it closed up again. The lesson was limpid: freedom is not the absence
of structure, but the rightness of the structure.
Then the text kept writing itself as the stick keeps turning. Without a prior plan. Without
chapters. Without artificial pauses. Tome 4 could not be anything other than a flow. A flow
held. An embodied flow. Any paragraph would have been a useless cut. Any intermediate title would have
frozen what had to remain mobile. The stick does not like blind angles. It prefers continuous
curves.
And so do I, from now on.
There is a moment when I understood that the stick was no longer teaching me only to move, but to
inhabit the interval. The interval between two gestures. Between two thoughts. Between two decisions. This
minuscule space that one habitually fills with anticipation, fear, or control.
With the stick, this interval becomes perceptible. One feels it in the light floating before the
trajectory reverses, in this precise point where inertia is no longer carried by the shoulder and not
yet taken up again by the wrist. There, there is nothing to do. Only let do. And this “letting” is
not an abandonment, it is an active confidence.
In this interval, thought changes nature once more. It stops being narrative. It becomes
topological. It no longer tells a story, it configures a space. I no longer think in
terms of causes and effects, but in terms of fields, tensions, thresholds. An idea no longer
chases another away, they coexist like possible orbits. Some move away,
others come closer, some enter into resonance. Nothing is rejected. Everything is weighed. This is
an internal ecology of thought.
That is where I recognized, with an almost troubling clarity, that this mode of functioning
was not new. It was simply covered over. Covered over by years of over-adaptation to
systems that value quick response, conclusion, visible performance. The stick
removes these layers one by one. It destroys nothing. It unclutters. And the more it unclutters, the more
the body recovers an ancient intelligence, patient, incredibly precise. An intelligence that
does not seek to convince, but to hold.
Hold the movement. Hold the trajectory. Hold the coherence.
At this stage, I stopped asking myself whether what I was living had to be shared, transmitted, theorized.
The question no longer made sense. What holds naturally seeks to propagate itself, not by
proselytism, but by resonance. Those who will recognize this state will recognize it
immediately, because they have already brushed against it somewhere — in a gesture, a practice, a
moment of grace without a name. The others will perhaps see nothing in it. And that is very well so. The
stick does not teach everyone. It does not seduce. It waits.
I also understood that this practice had put an end to a subtle confusion: that between intensity and
depth. Intensity tires. Depth stabilizes. With the stick, I could be fully
engaged without being saturated. The body worked, the breath adjusted, but the inside stayed clear.
This is exactly the opposite of what many contemporary devices produce: little
bodily engagement, much mental overload. Here, the load is displaced. It
comes back down. It anchors. And the psyche breathes.
Then AI, in this landscape, finds its right place. It becomes a surface tool in the service of a
depth already there. It is no longer summoned to compensate for a lack, but to prolong
a state. When I come back to it after the stick, I see immediately what is right and what is not
right. The useless sentences fall away. The artificial structures reveal themselves. Writing becomes
denser, simpler, more alive. The stick sharpened discernment without ever passing through
judgment.
I am beginning to see clearly that what I today call “life-process” is not a
philosophical abstraction, but a discipline of attention. A way of staying attuned to what
transforms itself, without seeking to capture it. The stick is a school of this discipline. It
teaches without explaining. It corrects without punishing. It opens without promising. And above all, it does not lie
ever. If I am elsewhere, it shows it. If I am present, it aligns.
This is perhaps, at bottom, the heart of this tome. To show that the most advanced human cognition
is not the one that piles up layers of complexity, but the one that knows how to come back to
simple, fundamental, embodied operators. A stick. A gravity. A body. An attention. And from
there, everything can be rebuilt, not in a heroic mode, but in a habitable mode.
Habitable for oneself.
Habitable for the others.
Habitable to last.
