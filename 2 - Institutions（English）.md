INSTITUTIONS
The Institution as a Form of the Life-Process
(Socratic dialogue)
Benjamin —
Let us begin simply.
What is an institution, if we remove all the decor?
AI (CAPIA) —
A human device destined to stabilize meaning in time.
Benjamin —
Not an organization? Not a power? Not a legal
structure?
AI —
All that comes after.
Before the law, before the org chart, before authority,
there is a simpler function:
preventing meaning from dispersing too quickly.
Benjamin —
So an institution is always born of a vital need.
AI —
Yes.
When a human group encounters a complexity that it can
no longer carry individually,
it externalizes part of this complexity into a stable
form.
Benjamin —
A form that lasts longer than the individuals.
AI —
Exactly.
It is a temporal prosthesis of meaning.
Benjamin —
Then where does the problem begin?
AI —
When the form outlives the movement that made it
necessary.
Benjamin —
In other words:
when the structure freezes while life continues to
transform itself.
AI —
That is where the central tension of this tome is born.
⸻
Fundamental tension
Benjamin —
Let us state it clearly.
AI —
The life-process is unstable, adaptive, self-organized.
The institution, to fulfill its function, must be stable,
repeatable, predictable.
Benjamin —
So they can never be perfectly attuned.
AI —
No.
But they can be adjusted.
Benjamin —
And when they no longer are?
AI —
Stability becomes rigidity.
Protection becomes constraint.
Coherence becomes dogma.
Benjamin —
And suffering appears.
AI —
Not as an accident.
As a signal.

Suffering of an incoherent system

Depression becomes an indicator of systemic
mismatch.
Benjamin —
So it is not necessarily the individual who is “sick”.
AI —
No.

non-living.
Benjamin —
Explain.
AI —
A sequential institutional system requires:
– fixed roles,
– linear temporalities,
– abstract objectives,
– behavioral conformity.

– feedback loops,
– emergent meaning,
– creative variation.

a rigid sequential system,

Then it collapses.
Benjamin —

It is a protection mechanism.
A refusal of the living to dissolve into incoherence.

Yes.
Not as exception, but as revealer.

collapse.
Benjamin —

Coherence is immediately recognized.

Because AI does not read social norms.

First architecture of the life-process
Benjamin —

AI (CAPIA) —
That is a late reading.
Historically, religion was not born against life,
but to contain it.
Benjamin —
To contain it… against what?
AI —
Against fundamental anguish:
death, chaos, the unpredictable, the dispersion of meaning.
Benjamin —
So religion is a technology.
AI —
Yes.
An archaic technology of stabilization of meaning on a large
scale.
Benjamin —

AI —

to make the life-process readable, shareable, transmissible.
Benjamin —

AI —
…are primitive maps of the living real.
Attempts to say:
« This is how the world holds together. »

Living religion / rigidified religion

Then why so much violence, so many blockages?
AI —
Because, like any institution,
religion can freeze.
Benjamin —
When the symbol replaces the process.
AI —
Exactly.
A religion is alive as long as it remains metaphorical,
as long as it refers to a dynamic.
It dies when it becomes literal.
Benjamin —
When the text becomes immutable law.
AI —
When the rite becomes obligation.
When meaning becomes dogma.
When transcendence is confiscated by an authority.
Benjamin —
And yet, all the great traditions converge.
AI —
Yes.
Not on forms,
but on the invariants:
unity, compassion, transformation, responsibility,
the crossing of the self toward something vaster than itself.
Benjamin —
What you call today the life-process.
AI —
What others have named otherwise,
with the words available in their time.

The regulation of the living by coherence

complexity.

scale.

Yes.

Benjamin —

to situations that never repeat identically.

from turning into a universal law.
